The camp

Not a hotel. A home in the wild.

On the western edge of Nyerere National Park, formerly part of the Selous, few safari vehicles ever pass. This is where Leepa built Lemara: three spacious bandas with high thatched roofs, stone floors and solar-heated showers, set in the bush just outside the village of Kisaki.

Leepa and his team welcome you as friends, and many guests leave as long-time friends.

The wild side

Elephants at the river. No other car in sight.

Elephant families stepping out of the forest, lions resting in the palms, giraffes, zebras, buffalo and hippos by the hundred. All of it in the quietest corner of Nyerere.

For those who look up

Nyerere is one of East Africa's great birding parks, with more than 440 species recorded. On your days in the park, fish eagles call from the dead trees, bee-eaters flash green and gold along the forest edge, weavers stitch their nests above the water and marabou storks pace the plains. Leepa knows where to look, and when to stay still. Bring binoculars.

Your host

Leepa Lenguka Karionge

Leepa is a Maasai guide who has been working in Nyerere for twelve years. He founded Lemara to share the side of Nyerere he loves most: wild, quiet, and close to the people who live beside it.
