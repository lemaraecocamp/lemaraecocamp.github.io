Leepa & Kisaki

The people behind Lemara.

A Maasai guide, his team, and the community of Kisaki, at the edge of the wild.

Your host

Leepa Lenguka Karionge

Leepa is a Maasai guide who has been working in Nyerere for twelve years. He knows the western side of the park as few others do: where the elephants come to drink, which plants heal, how to walk quietly enough to hear the forest.

“We founded Lemara to break the traditional safari circuit in Tanzania. We dreamed of a place where travelers could take the time to meet us, exchange and discover the beauty of our nature, from the infinitely large to the infinitely small.”

Leepa Lenguka Karionge, founder

Our vision

A safari is a time to open your senses.

We do not believe in safaris spent chasing animals. Like any journey, a safari is a time to open your senses to the beauty of our animals, trees, hills and plains. A time to understand the balance of our ecosystem, and how fragile it is.

And a time to meet the people who know how to live in harmony with this land, and to learn from them that we, too, can be part of this world without harming it.

The community

The Maasai of Kisaki.

Lemara stands beside the village where Leepa's community lives. Guests are welcome into daily life at its own pace, and sometimes into its celebrations, always as guests.

Beyond tourism

What Lemara gives back.

Today, Lemara supports dozens of people. No income leaves Tanzania: the camp is Tanzanian-run, buys its food and supplies in Kisaki, and brings visitors to discover village life. Beyond the camp, Leepa leads youth associations in the community, helping young people of Kisaki build their future close to home.

“From the moment of our arrival, Leepa made us feel as if we were at home.”
