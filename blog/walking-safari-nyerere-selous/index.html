Walking safari in Nyerere: what to expect

A practical guide from Leepa and the Lemara team.

← All articles

From a vehicle, you watch the bush. On foot, you are part of it. A walking safari is one of the reasons travellers choose Nyerere over the busier parks of the north. Here is what it is really like.

How it works

At Lemara, walking safaris take place inside Nyerere National Park, during a full-day game drive. You drive into the park in the morning, then leave the vehicle for about two hours on foot, before continuing the day by 4x4.

Every walk is led by an armed park ranger, with your guide alongside. The group stays small, walks in single file and moves quietly.

What you see on foot

Walking is less about big animals and more about everything you miss from a car:

  • Tracks and signs: the round print of an elephant, the drag mark of a crocodile, a lion's path in the sand. Your guide teaches you to read them.
  • The small life: dung beetles, termite mounds, spiders' webs, birds' nests.
  • Plants and trees: which bark heals, which fruit the baboons eat, which tree the elephants strip.
  • Sounds and smells: the alarm call of an impala, the musk of a buffalo herd nearby.

And sometimes, the big ones: giraffes watching you from the trees, a herd of elephants at a safe distance. Meeting them on foot, at their level, is something you do not forget.

Is it safe?

Yes, when it is done properly. The ranger knows the animals and the terrain, chooses the route and keeps a safe distance. Your role is simple: listen to the briefing, stay together, stay quiet, and follow instructions without discussion.

Who can join?

Walking safaris suit anyone in reasonable health who can walk for two hours at a gentle pace in the heat. Children can join from the age of 12. Younger children can enjoy the game drives, the boat safari and the forest walk with Leepa.

What to wear and bring

  • Clothes in neutral colours: khaki, green, brown. Avoid white and bright colours.
  • Closed walking shoes and long trousers.
  • A hat, sunscreen and a water bottle.
  • Binoculars, and a camera without a noisy shutter if you can.

The forest walk with Leepa

Outside the park, around the camp, Leepa also leads forest walks. There is no big game here, but plenty of stories: the roots and leaves of Maasai traditional medicine, the tracks of small animals, the sounds of the forest. It is the perfect first afternoon at Lemara.
