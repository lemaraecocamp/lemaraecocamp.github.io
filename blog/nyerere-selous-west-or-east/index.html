Nyerere West or East: which side of Selous to choose?

A practical guide from Leepa and the Lemara team.

← All articles

Nyerere National Park, formerly the Selous Game Reserve, is East Africa's largest park, more than twice the size of the Serengeti. Where you stay inside it shapes your whole safari. Here is an honest comparison of its two main sides, from the people who live on the quieter one.

The east: lakes, lodges and day-trippers

The eastern sector, around Mtemere gate, the Rufiji River and the lakes of Manze and Siwandu, is where most visitors go. It is beautiful, with palm-fringed lakes and a high density of wildlife. It is also where most lodges are, and where the light aircraft from Zanzibar land, often for a single day.

Choose the east if you have only one or two nights, and do not mind sharing a sighting with other vehicles.

The west: quiet, forest and walking

The western sector, around Matambwe gate and the village of Kisaki, sees far fewer visitors. The landscape mixes miombo woodland, forest, open plains, hot springs and rivers, with the Uluguru hills on the horizon. You can drive for hours without meeting another car.

Choose the west if you want space and silence, to walk as much as to drive, and to meet the people who live beside the park.

And the lakes? From the west, the lakes are within reach. We often go there with our guests, including to lakes that few visitors ever see, and to a few secret places we keep for them.

Side by side

East (Mtemere) West (Matambwe, Kisaki)
Visitors Most of the park's visitors Very few
Landscape Lakes, river, palms Forest, plains, hot springs, hills, and the lakes within reach
Wildlife Very dense around the lakes Elephants, lions, giraffes, buffalo, hippos, in a wilder setting
Access Light aircraft, long road Train (SGR or TAZARA), road, airstrip
Accommodation Many lodges, often high-end A handful of small camps
Culture Little contact with villages Kisaki village and its Maasai community next door

Our honest view

If seeing the largest number of animals in the shortest time is your only goal, the east is hard to beat. But if you want to feel the wild, to hear it at night, walk in it with a ranger and share it with almost no one, the west is the better choice. And staying in the west does not mean giving up the lakes: we often drive there, including to lakes that almost no one else sees.

Stay in the west with Leepa

Lemara is a small camp of three bandas on the edge of the western sector, run by Leepa, a Maasai guide who has worked in Nyerere for twelve years. From here, game drives, walking safaris and boat safaris explore the quiet side of the park.
