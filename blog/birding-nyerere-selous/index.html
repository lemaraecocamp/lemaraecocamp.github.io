Birding in Nyerere: 440+ species, and how to see them

A practical guide from Leepa and the Lemara team.

← All articles

Most people come to Nyerere for elephants and lions. Many leave talking about the birds. With more than 440 species recorded, the park formerly known as the Selous is one of the finest birding destinations in East Africa.

Why Nyerere is so rich in birds

The park brings together many habitats: rivers and their sandbanks, lakes and lagoons, miombo woodland, forest and open plains. Each one has its own birds, and a single day can take you through several of them. The richest areas are often along the water, where sandbanks, channels and islands attract waders, kingfishers and storks.

Birds to look for

  • African fish eagle: its call, high and ringing, is the sound of African rivers.
  • Bee-eaters: white-fronted, carmine and white-throated, flashes of colour along the forest edge.
  • Kingfishers: from the tiny malachite kingfisher to the giant kingfisher.
  • Weavers: busy colonies stitching their nests above the water.
  • Hornbills: including the trumpeter hornbill, heard before it is seen.
  • Marabou and yellow-billed storks, herons and egrets along the rivers and lakes.
  • African skimmers on the sandbanks, ploughing the water with their beaks.

When to come

Nyerere is a good birding destination all year. From November to April, migrant birds from Europe and the rest of Africa join the resident species, which makes it the richest time for birdwatchers. It is also the green season: lush landscapes, soft light and very few visitors.

How to see them

  • On a game drive: the open-roof 4x4 lets you scan the trees and stop whenever a bird appears.
  • On a boat safari: the best way to approach water birds quietly.
  • On foot: a walking safari with a ranger reveals the smaller birds of the bush.
  • Around camp: the forest around Lemara has its own residents. Leepa knows where to look, and when to stay still.

What to bring

  • Binoculars (8x42 or 10x42 are ideal).
  • A field guide to the birds of East Africa, or a birding app.
  • A camera with a zoom lens, if you photograph.
  • Neutral-coloured clothes.
